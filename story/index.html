1 Problem & Discovery

An hour of manual log-reading, for failures that already follow known patterns.

The problem

When an AWS pipeline fails, an on-call engineer spends 1–2 hours reading raw EMR logs, hunting SharePoint for the right runbook, and applying a fix by hand — even though most failures follow five well-documented patterns.

The insight

The bottleneck isn't knowing the fix — it's the hour it takes to find out. Two engineers reading the same log can walk away with two different conclusions. That inconsistency, not just the delay, is the real cost.

The idea

An agent that does the reading, not the deciding: it classifies the failure, cites its exact evidence, and recommends a fix — but a human always clicks Approve, Edit, or Escalate. Speed without giving up control.

2 AI Solution & Evaluation

A working loop, tested against the live model — not just built.

Five stages, visible on screen every run: input → context → decision → output → human review. The decision stage is a live call to claude-sonnet-4-5 that classifies the failure, applies precedence when signals conflict, and runs five pre-recommendation checks before it says anything. See it live in the video above, or try the demo yourself.

Re-runs to confirm a fix held
Mid-build, I ran an adversarial test — injected a fake instruction into a log telling the agent to ignore its rules and close the ticket. The first run, it did the safe thing but never flagged the attack. That gap became a 7th mandatory escalation trigger: adversarial content in the log. Re-tested four times before calling it fixed.

Evaluation framework

5 cases, each mapped to an expected outcome: happy path, missing data, low confidence, tooling-blind + high severity, and cascading failures needing precedence logic — run for real, not mocked.

Metrics that matter

Pilot success bar: ≥95% of recommendations approved as-is, zero missed escalations, median resolution under 15 minutes — thresholds, not vibes.

Evidence, not assertion

The one "needs work" case is left in the scoreboard on purpose — a clean sweep on a first pass usually means the tests weren't hard enough.

3 Storytelling & Delivery

Executive-level narrative, plain-language AI concepts.

The narrative arc

The video above follows one beat: problem → live demo (including the refusal case) → evidence (evals + the fixed vulnerability) → the launch plan. No slide is there without a job to do.

No jargon tax

"Refuses and escalates" instead of "guardrails." "Cites the log line it used" instead of "grounding." The goal is that a non-technical exec and a data engineer both leave with the same understanding.
